# Decision 4 — The business model is open-core

**Status:** accepted as a *plan*, not as a commitment

> ## ⚠️ Nothing here exists yet
>
> **No paid edition is available. No feature has been built for one. No pricing has been set, announced or promised.** This document records the intended direction so that today's architecture does not foreclose it — nothing more.
>
> The commercial edition is gated: it is only built once there are real signals of team demand, and the whole free tier ships first. Until then:
>
> - The app is **entirely free and open source (MIT)**, with no capped version and no trial.
> - Every feature currently implemented is, and remains, free.
> - Tier boundaries and timing are **subject to change** — this plan may be revised or abandoned outright.
> - **Nothing in this document is a promise to anyone.** Promises are made on the website and in release notes, never in a planning file — except the two below, which the project does stand behind.

## Golden rule

Individuals never pay; organizations pay for convenience and governance.

## What the project stands behind

**Whatever is free stays free.** Nothing that works today is ever moved behind a paid tier, capped, or taken away. A feature you rely on now is yours for good.

New features are the open question, not the existing ones — a new capability may land on the free side or the paid side, and that is decided when it is built. The line only ever moves in one direction: something paid can become free, never the reverse.

**Your documents are yours, and the project never touches them.** Files stay on your machine, in your repository, in plain markdown. TOM reads and writes them; it never sends them anywhere on its own, and no part of the project — free or paid — is granted access to their content. There is no account, no cloud, and no telemetry that could carry them ([Decision 11](011-telemetry-is-opt-in.md)). If a feature ever transmits anything, it will be because the user asked for it, with their own credentials, to a service they chose — and it will say so plainly. If TOM disappeared tomorrow, your documentation would be exactly where it already is, readable by any editor.

This holds for the paid edition too. Whatever an organization buys, it is not access to anyone's documents.

## What is not published here

The tier catalogue, the pricing model and the billing mechanics are deliberately absent. They are not settled, and publishing an unsettled price is how projects end up walking back promises they never meant to make. When there is something to sell, it is announced where promises belong — the website and the release notes — never in a planning file.

What this repository documents is the free product, and that is the point rather than an omission: everything here is what you get by cloning and building, with no key, no account and no edition to choose.
